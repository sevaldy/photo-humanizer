# FUSE: optional experimental directions

S7: FUSE 1-20, Neville Brody and Jon Wozencroft (TASCHEN, 2012). A multi-contributor collection, not one Brody style or an information-design textbook. Guidance is our adaptation of selected inspected works, not rules claimed from the entire collection. Use with General and Typography; no mandatory FUSE look.

## Derive a concept, not a surface imitation

Tie a visual operation to the message. Fragmentation may support interrupted forms, modularity repeated units, tension compressed scale or collision. These are possible concepts, not automatic mappings. Quiet design remains available.

## Apply with freedom and purpose

Select an expressive operation or compatible set rather than every effect. Scale, cropping, orientation, fragmentation, layering, blur, and irregular rhythm may serve the composition. No mandatory palette, font, density, or operation.

Separate expressive and informational layers where needed. They can interact: a headline may act as an image while supporting text supplies a stable reading path. Degree of experimentation follows the brief; conceptual art and dense informative posters have different demands.

Do not equate FUSE with arbitrary chaos, generic grunge, unreadable copy, or compulsory 1990s styling. A successful adaptation has a concept and works at viewing size; attractive texture alone does not satisfy an informative brief.

## Review

Does the experiment add meaning or useful attention? Is essential content findable? Are the graphic relationships intentional? Adjust a failing region/operation rather than automatically flattening the entire design into a generic template.
