# Reference map: General / Photo / Poster

Prepared 31 October 2026. Original operational summaries based on selected supplied pages, not an exhaustive reading or a reproduction of the books. Printed page numbers and one-based PDF page numbers are distinguished below. An application is our editorial adaptation unless explicitly described as an observed device.

## General - S5: Graphic Design School, seventh edition

David Dabner, Sandra Stewart, Abbie Vickress, 2020. Selected text inspected at printed pp.20, 24, 32-33, 36-37, 40-41, 44-47, 78-79, 92-93, 96-97 (PDF pages = printed +2). Visual samples of composition, grid, emphasis, and color pages were also inspected. Selection deliberately excludes app/web implementation, career chapters, and software-specific walkthroughs from the skill.

| Principle and source | When useful | Application example | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience/message, pp.20, 24 (PDF 22, 26) | Choosing what the visual must communicate | Translate a spoken explanation into a self-contained informative message | Does not prescribe one audience-research ritual per task |
| Composition as relationships, pp.32-33 (PDF 34-35) | Every visual | Place subject/message and support so their relative importance is apparent | Does not require a minimalist layout |
| Positive/negative space, pp.36-37 (PDF 38-39) | Subject separation, type/image interaction | Shape a quiet region around essential text or a product edge | Thirds/geometric proportions are tools, not mandatory pass criteria |
| Layout and grid, pp.40-41 (PDF 42-43) | Organizing different content blocks | Align explanation, diagram, and caption using a flexible underlying structure | No universal column count; grid may be broken deliberately |
| Symmetry/asymmetry, pp.44-45 (PDF 46-47) | Selecting tone and visual balance | Center an invitation or offset an editorial headline | Neither arrangement is automatically superior |
| Pace/contrast, pp.46-47 (PDF 48-49) | Complex posters or a requested series | Alternate emphasis and quieter explanation while retaining common roles | Single posters need not imitate page-turn pacing |
| Emphasis, pp.78-79 (PDF 80-81) | Distinguishing levels of information | Emphasize the key lesson with space/weight rather than every effect at once | Avoid turning less-is-more into a mandatory aesthetic |
| Color legibility, pp.92-93 (PDF 94-95) | Colored/photographic backgrounds | Test actual factual text against its background and reproduction context | Palette harmony does not prove readability; no automatic CMYK rule |
| Color as information, pp.96-97 (PDF 98-99) | Categories and diagrams | Pair category accents with clear labels and consistent grouping | Color is not a universal semantic code or the only identifier |

Mapped into GENERAL.md and the poster/content workflow. Transcription, summarization, the five-question budget, and tool execution are authored workflow choices, not book findings.

## Photo - S1-S4

Preserve the pre-existing photographic evidence map in SOURCES.md. S1 separates technical appearance from scene plausibility; S2/S3 provide inspection categories, not a detector recipe; S4 informs light-source size, reflection, glass, metal, and portrait texture. The three newly supplied design books do not replace specialist photographic references.

Applications: diagnose only visible defects; distinguish physical-scene issues from graphic effects; preserve requested finish; match grain locally when integration requires it. No universal realism score, camera-setting inference, or noise recipe.

## Poster typography - S6: Thinking with Type, third edition

Ellen Lupton, 2024. Selected text inspected at printed pp.58-59, 116, 124, 128, 132-134, 138, 148, 156-158, 160-161, 164, 222-223, 226, 236-237, 244 (PDF pages = printed +2); several were inspected visually as well. This is selected-section coverage, not the entire book. The book's other scripts and UI examples are not converted into requirements for Indonesian static posters.

| Principle and source | When useful | Application example | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type selection, pp.58-59 (PDF 60-61) | Defining poster voice | Distinctive display headline with comfortable information text | Dense explanatory work may need body text tested first; no universal font count |
| Alignment/meaningful breaks, p.116 (PDF 118) | Short/centered text | Keep event phrases and names in coherent lines | Centering is available, not a compulsory or forbidden style |
| Line length, p.124 (PDF 126) | Longer explanation | Adjust column width with face, size, and leading | Suggested body-text ranges are not headline limits |
| Short lines, p.128 (PDF 130) | Stranded words and awkward blocks | Rephrase permitted copy or adjust a break before shrinking everything | Exact user wording may instead require layout changes |
| Kerning/tracking, pp.132-134 (PDF 134-136) | Large words and overall text texture | Correct a conspicuous display gap; tune capitals locally | No arbitrary global tracking value |
| Leading, p.138 (PDF 140) | Text block rhythm | Compact headline and comfortably spaced explanation | Book/screen defaults are starting points, not fixed poster ratios |
| Legibility/readability, p.148 (PDF 150) | Actual viewing context | Check glyph clarity and comprehension at intended size | Typeface category alone cannot guarantee either |
| Minimal/layered hierarchy, pp.156-161, selected (PDF 158-163) | Information priority | Use position and a few weights, or stronger layered contrast, according to the brief | No requirement that every poster be either Swiss minimal or maximal |
| Type scales, p.164 (PDF 166) | Coordinating sizes | Use a scale if it helps relationships | Mathematical ratios do not certify quality |
| Grouping/alignment, pp.222-223, 226 (PDF 224-225, 228) | Labels, diagrams, captions | Make caption-image association and group boundaries apparent | Optical alignment may differ from bounding-box alignment |
| Modular grid, pp.236-237 (PDF 238-239) | Related text/image zones | Coordinate blocks without identical placement | Grid breaks remain available |
| Serial design, p.244 (PDF 246) | A requested poster family | Carry type roles across varied topic-specific compositions | Do not silently turn a one-poster brief into a series |

Mapped into TYPOGRAPHY.md and COMPOSITES-POSTERS.md. The choice to preserve fact meaning while freely rewriting transcript copy is our task-specific adaptation.

## Poster exploration - S7: FUSE 1-20

Overview samples: PDF 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 25, 28, 31, 36, 40, 50, 60, 72, 80, 86, 110, 120, 146, 160, 180, 224, 300, 390. Detailed worked examples below were viewed as individual rendered pages. This is sampling, not full OCR or a claim to have read all essays.

The scan's page offsets vary. Use the verified per-page locations rather than a single conversion formula.

| Observed sample | Location | Adaptation into our skill | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| FUSE 1 overview: scale and blurred background forms | PDF 28; printed number not visible | A separate typographic depth plane can contrast with foreground content | Do not use background blur as a required style |
| Phil Baines, Can You...?: reduced letter fragments | PDF 31; printed 26 | A short display motif can use partial recognition | Essential facts still need an appropriate reading route |
| Malcolm Garrett, Stealth: geometric glyph construction | PDF 36; printed 31 | Repeated construction logic can unify related graphic marks | Coherent variation, not random replacement characters |
| Max Kisman, Linear Konstrukt: angular Roman-derived forms | PDF 50; printed 45 | Derive a concept-specific graphic vocabulary | Do not assume unfamiliar letterforms are suitable for long instructions |
| Barry Deck, Caustic Biomorph: irregular silhouettes | PDF 86; printed 81 | Explore organic display character when the topic/tone supports it | No copied alphabet or default distress filter |

Mapped into FUSE.md. Our separation of expressive and informational roles is an adaptation for informative posters; it is not a universal rule attributed to every FUSE contributor. No fixed Brody look, palette, technique, or nostalgia requirement follows from these samples.
