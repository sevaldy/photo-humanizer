# General: shared visual standards

Apply across posters, photographs, and composites. These are decision standards, not a mandatory minimal style. Basis: S5, selected composition/color sections; photographic checks additionally use S1-S4. Page-level adaptations are in [SOURCE-MAP](references/SOURCE-MAP.md).

## Brief and content

Identify what the viewer should understand, notice, or do; the medium and viewing size; and which elements must stay faithful. Resolve important gaps with the question budget in SKILL.md. Choose incidental styling rather than asking the user to art-direct every detail.

Separate source meaning from presentation. For transcripts, an internal fact outline may retain timestamps/sections for verification; do not print them by default. Remove spoken repetition and organize related ideas. Preserve negation, uncertainty, scope, sequence, units, and who said what. Opinions remain opinions and examples remain examples. Graphic emphasis must not turn a qualified statement into an absolute claim.

## Composition that supports the purpose

- **Hierarchy:** decide the main subject/message and the next level of attention. Hierarchy may be subtle or emphatic.
- **Balance:** distribute visual weight through size, position, density, tone, and space. Symmetry, asymmetry, and centered layouts are all available.
- **Relationships:** use proximity, alignment, repetition, and differences to express grouping. In photos this may mean subject separation rather than columns.
- **Space:** shape surrounding and internal empty areas deliberately. Negative space can clarify, create tension, or describe a silhouette; it need not be white or abundant.
- **Contrast:** choose differences that serve the focal sequence. Avoid accidental competition or indistinguishable levels; preserve purposeful tonal subtlety.
- **Coherence:** photographic parts should share believable relationships when they occupy one scene. Graphic elements may intentionally use different scale or perspective.

Grids, thirds, modular scales, and geometric proportions are optional working tools. Do not impose a golden ratio, fixed column count, mandatory card layout, fixed font count, or universal spacing recipe. Select structure from content and viewing context.

## Color and finish

Give colors useful roles: base, subject, supporting information, emphasis, or category. No required palette size. When color encodes meaning, pair it with labels or another visible distinction where needed. Check actual text/background combinations, including photographs and textured fields; palette harmony alone does not ensure readability.

For photos, preserve requested grade and subject fidelity. For posters, allow bold combinations when information remains usable. Do not claim precise product-color matching from uncalibrated references or compare unlike illumination as though measuring material color.

Judge polish at intended use size. Do not smooth away source character, add artificial flaws, or remove intentional effects to satisfy an assumed aesthetic.

## Shared review

Ask internally: does the output do the requested job; are required facts/assets present; is the main message/subject clear; are local details consistent with the style; does it work at actual viewing size? Then inspect relevant details at larger scale. Fix the most consequential issue first.

An informative poster, expressive event graphic, and clean product photo can pass these standards with very different appearances. The invariant is intentional communication and faithful execution of the brief.
